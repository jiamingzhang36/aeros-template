## Content Summary
Journal of Economic Perspectives review of the economic theory and evidence on sugar-sweetened beverage taxes.

## Research Question
How should economists evaluate SSB taxes given externalities, internalities, regressivity, demand response, and health benefits?

## Data
Review and synthesis of theoretical and empirical SSB tax evidence.

## Treatment / Exposure / Shock
Sugar-sweetened beverage excise taxes, not SNAP purchase restrictions.

## Outcomes
Demand response, pass-through, regressivity, health externalities/internalities, and welfare-relevant distributional incidence.

## Method / Identification
Economic theory and literature synthesis.

## Key Assumptions

## Main Findings
Distributional incidence is central to evaluating food and beverage policies; policy burden and corrective benefits may differ across income groups.

## Limitations
Tax evidence is not evidence on in-kind SNAP restrictions, retailer implementation, or actual MAHA waiver purchase responses.
