## Content Summary
Empirical paper on consumption responses to in-kind food transfers using Food Stamp Program introduction.

## Research Question
How do households respond to the introduction of an in-kind food transfer?

## Data
Historical county-level rollout of the Food Stamp Program in the 1960s and early 1970s linked to the Panel Study of Income Dynamics.

## Treatment / Exposure / Shock
Program introduction of food stamps.

## Outcomes
Out-of-pocket food spending and total food expenditures.

## Method / Identification
Difference-in-differences using county rollout timing and household consumption data.

## Key Assumptions
Use for conceptual in-kind-transfer framing, not as evidence on current waivers.

## Main Findings
AEA abstract reports that food stamps reduce out-of-pocket food spending and increase overall food expenditures, with inframarginal households responding similarly to cash and food stamps.

## Limitations
Historical rollout evidence on benefit introduction is not evidence on modern state product restrictions or MAHA waiver implementation.
