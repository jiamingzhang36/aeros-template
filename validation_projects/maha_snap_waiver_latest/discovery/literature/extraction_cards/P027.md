## Content Summary
Marketing Science article using household purchase data to study whether temporary healthy-food incentives in WIC lead to persistent changes.

## Research Question
Do temporary incentives for healthier purchases produce persistent changes in household food-purchasing behavior?

## Data
Household-level food-purchase data linked to WIC policy incentives.

## Treatment / Exposure / Shock
Implemented WIC incentive/benefit-design change, not a SNAP food restriction waiver.

## Outcomes
Food purchase behavior during and after the incentive period.

## Method / Identification
Empirical analysis of household scanner data around a WIC benefit-design setting.

## Key Assumptions
Use only within the bounded literature function recorded in this card.

## Main Findings
Implemented benefit designs can affect food purchasing, but the object is actual purchase response after implementation rather than ex-ante exposure.

## Limitations
WIC population and benefit design differ from SNAP restriction waivers; use as contrast, not as direct evidence.

## What This Paper Can Support
- Household scanner data can be used for implemented nutrition-program purchase-response research.
- Benefit-design changes are a natural frame for food-program policy analysis.

## What This Paper Cannot Support
- Does not show MAHA waiver purchase effects.
- Does not justify estimating demand or persistence with 2022--2024 pre-policy MAHA data.
