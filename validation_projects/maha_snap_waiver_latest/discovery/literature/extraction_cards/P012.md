## Content Summary
Randomized trial of incentives and purchase restrictions in a food benefit program.

## Research Question
Do incentives and purchase restrictions affect lower-income households' food and beverage purchases?

## Data
Lower-income adults in Minneapolis-St. Paul; 279 randomized, 252 analytic sample with receipt data.

## Treatment / Exposure / Shock
Study-specific food benefit debit card with incentive, restriction, incentive-plus-restriction, or control rules.

## Outcomes
Weekly spending on fruits, vegetables, SSBs, sweet baked goods, candy, total grocery, and restaurant purchases.

## Method / Identification
Randomized trial with receipt-based purchase measurement and general linear models.

## Key Assumptions
Experimental debit-card setting provides a policy-basket analogue but is not equivalent to real SNAP waiver implementation.

## Main Findings
Restrictions reduced SSB and sweet baked-good spending relative to control; fruit purchases rose in the incentive-plus-restriction group.

## Limitations
Short duration, local experimental setting, not state waiver implementation, and participants were not current SNAP users.
