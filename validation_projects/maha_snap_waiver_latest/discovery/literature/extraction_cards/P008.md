## Content Summary
ERS analysis explaining why SNAP SSB restrictions may not map directly to consumption reductions.

## Research Question
Would restricting SSB purchases with SNAP benefits likely reduce SSB consumption?

## Data
NHANES dietary-intake and household food-spending evidence summarized by ERS.

## Treatment / Exposure / Shock
Hypothetical SNAP exclusion of SSB purchases.

## Outcomes
SSB consumption patterns, benefit-food spending gap, and cash substitution logic.

## Method / Identification
Descriptive and adjusted dietary-intake analysis plus spending-benefit comparison.

## Key Assumptions
SNAP benefits are only part of household food resources; own cash can substitute for restricted purchases.

## Main Findings
After adjusting for characteristics, SNAP participants were no more likely to consume SSBs than similar low-income nonparticipants, and most SNAP households spent more on food than their SNAP benefit.

## Limitations
Pre-MAHA evidence; does not measure current waiver baskets or implementation.
