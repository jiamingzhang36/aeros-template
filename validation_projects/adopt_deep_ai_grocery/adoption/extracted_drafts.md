## Idea

Audit AI-generated grocery recommendations as a form of dynamic information customization in food choice. The project treats AI food assistants as algorithmic intermediaries that interpret, rank, and construct grocery baskets rather than merely disclosing nutrition information.

## Research Question

Do AI-generated grocery recommendations produce baskets that are stable, affordable, nutritionally reasonable, constraint-compliant, feasible, and equitable across realistic household constraints, especially for low-income and SNAP-relevant food choice environments?

## Candidate Claims

- AI grocery assistants represent a shift from static nutrition disclosure to algorithmic intermediation in food choice.
- The same consumer profile may receive different recommendations across repeated interactions, models, or prompts.
- AI-generated grocery advice may reduce search costs and cognitive burden but may also produce unstable, expensive, unrealistic, or unequal recommendations.
- Evaluating AI food advice requires outcomes beyond healthfulness, including affordability, feasibility, budget compliance, SNAP relevance, transparency, and equity.
- The contribution should be framed narrowly as an audit of AI-generated grocery baskets under realistic food policy constraints, not as the first study of AI nutrition advice.

## Methods

- Audit study of AI-generated grocery baskets.
- Observation unit: persona-model-prompt-repeated run combination.
- Persona-based prompting strategy covering household constraints such as income, budget, health condition, cooking capacity, language, shopping access, and SNAP-like eligibility.
- Repeated runs to measure stability and reproducibility.
- Comparison across AI models and prompts.
- Outcome measurement for stability, budget compliance, healthfulness, affordability, feasibility, transparency, and equity.
- No human-subject experiment proposed for the first stage.

## Data / Results Signals

- Main proposed data are AI-generated grocery baskets.
- Each basket would be generated from combinations of persona, model, prompt, and repeated run.
- No completed empirical results are reported in the supplied excerpt.
- The memo positions the first-stage design as faster than a discrete choice experiment and potentially avoiding immediate IRB needs because it audits AI outputs directly.

## Risks

- Risk of overclaiming novelty by saying this is the first study of AI nutrition advice.
- AI outputs may be non-reproducible or unstable.
- Recommendations may violate user constraints such as budget, SNAP eligibility, cooking capacity, food access, or health needs.
- A nutritionally sound recommendation may be policy-irrelevant if it is too expensive, unavailable, not SNAP-eligible, or hard to prepare.
- Existing literature on AI nutrition advice, LLM reliability, and bias must be reviewed before claiming a gap.
- Need to avoid converting exploratory ideas into formal conclusions without verification.
