## Content Summary
Medicaid simulated-eligibility paper used as methodological analogue for isolating policy-rule variation.

## Research Question
Do expansions in Medicaid eligibility for pregnant women improve birth outcomes?

## Data
State Medicaid rules, CPS simulation, and aggregate Vital Statistics, 1979-1990.

## Treatment / Exposure / Shock
Changes in state Medicaid eligibility rules.

## Outcomes
Simulated Medicaid eligibility, infant mortality, low birthweight, take-up/coverage.

## Method / Identification
Detailed state-rule simulation model linked to aggregate outcome data.

## Key Assumptions
Applying policy rules to a common data source can separate rule generosity from population composition.

## Main Findings
Eligibility expansions increased Medicaid eligibility at different rates across states and were associated with improvements in infant outcomes, especially targeted expansions.
