## Content Summary
WIC food-package revision study using low-income household purchase data.

## Research Question
Did federal nutrition program revisions change low-income households' food purchases?

## Data
Nielsen Homescan Consumer Panel purchases from 2008 to 2014 for 5,352 low-income households with preschoolers.

## Treatment / Exposure / Shock
WIC food package revisions.

## Outcomes
Packaged-food purchases, calories, sodium, total fat, sugar, encouraged/discouraged food groups, processing/convenience categories.

## Method / Identification
Longitudinal random-effects models comparing WIC-participating and income-eligible nonparticipating households.

## Key Assumptions
WIC package revisions provide a scanner-data precedent for rule-to-basket analysis, but WIC is not SNAP and the intervention was implemented rather than ex-ante only.

## Main Findings
Among WIC households, purchases of calories, sodium, total fat, and sugar fell, purchases of refined grains, grain-based desserts, higher-fat milks, and SSBs decreased, and purchases of fruits/vegetables without added sugar/fat/salt increased.

## Limitations
Focuses on packaged food purchases and WIC revisions; not a SNAP waiver or actual EBT product-restriction study.
