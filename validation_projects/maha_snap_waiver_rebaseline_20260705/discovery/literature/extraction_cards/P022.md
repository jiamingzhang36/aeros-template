## Content Summary
NBER working paper using a large randomized field experiment to study SNAP administrative burden in the application interview process.

## Research Question
Does reducing the burden of SNAP intake interviews increase approvals and participation?

## Data
Los Angeles County SNAP applicants applying through GetCalFresh; field experiment involving about 65,000 applicants.

## Treatment / Exposure / Shock
Access to flexible, applicant-initiated interviews rather than only standard scheduled interviews.

## Outcomes
Application approval by deadlines, early approval, longer-run SNAP participation, targeting/program-integrity checks, and related process outcomes.

## Method / Identification
Randomized field experiment with additional natural-experiment and rollout checks.

## Key Assumptions
Use only within the bounded literature function recorded in this card.

## Main Findings
Flexible interviews raised approvals by roughly six percentage points, doubled early approvals, and increased longer-run participation by over two percentage points.

## Limitations
Studies SNAP access and procedural denials, not SNAP food-purchase restrictions or retailer implementation.

## What This Paper Can Support
- SNAP administrative requirements can create meaningful procedural barriers.
- Implementation design and administrative flexibility can change realized program outcomes.

## What This Paper Cannot Support
- Does not estimate effects of food restriction waivers.
- Does not support a claim that USDA approval concentration is causal.

## Relevance To This Project
Supports the paper's mechanism language that administrative procedures can shape realized access and policy outcomes; it should not be used as evidence of MAHA purchase effects.
