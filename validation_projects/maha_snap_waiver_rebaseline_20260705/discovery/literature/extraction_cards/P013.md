## Content Summary
Randomized clinical trial on subsidies and prohibitions in a food benefit program.

## Research Question
Do incentives for fruits/vegetables and prohibitions on less nutritious foods improve diet quality in a food benefit program?

## Data
Lower-income adults not currently enrolled in SNAP; 279 randomized and 265 completed follow-up in the PMC author manuscript.

## Treatment / Exposure / Shock
Four experimental benefit conditions: incentive, restriction, incentive plus restriction, and control.

## Outcomes
Energy intake, discretionary calories, restricted food intake, fruits/vegetables, Healthy Eating Index, household food security, and exploratory BMI.

## Method / Identification
Randomized clinical trial with follow-up dietary outcomes and general linear model comparisons by treatment arm.

## Key Assumptions
Experimental food-benefit debit-card setting is an RCT benchmark for feasible restriction baskets, not evidence on MAHA waiver implementation.

## Main Findings
The incentive-plus-restriction arm produced several favorable changes relative to control, including lower energy intake, lower discretionary calories, lower intake of SSBs/sweet baked goods/candy, higher solid fruit intake, and higher Healthy Eating Index score; fewer improvements appeared in incentive-only or restriction-only arms.

## Limitations
Participants were lower-income but not current SNAP enrollees; the benefit card is not the EBT/POS waiver environment and does not identify MAHA state administrative selection.
